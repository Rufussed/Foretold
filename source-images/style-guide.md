# Fortold illustration style guide

Status: working draft.

## Artistic direction

Tarot-inspired, painterly fantasy with integrated elemental motifs and dark ink
outlining. The image should feel painted and drawn by hand: expressive,
theatrical, slightly strange, and rich in character.

Combine the symbolic clarity of an old tarot card with textured painted colour.
Use confident, irregular dark contours around faces, hands, clothing and important
objects. Let brushwork carry light, atmosphere and landscape. Simplify detail
where it helps the silhouette and expression read at card size.

Build forms from broad, coherent blocks of colour with a small number of clear
shadow and highlight shapes. Keep painterly texture subtle within those blocks;
avoid mottled tonal patchwork, scattered bright flecks and excessive small folds.
Ink contours carry much of the detail without breaking up the colour masses.

Avoid photographic skin, glossy digital rendering, smooth 3D surfaces and
cinematic realism. Antique character need not mean a uniformly brown or faded
image. Preserve strong colour, living paint and readable contrast.

### Old tarot drawing

Use old European tarot court cards, hand-coloured woodcuts and medieval book
illustration as the drawing language: irregular dark ink contours, slightly
naive but coherent anatomy, flattened forms and economical hatching beneath
broad painted colour. Prefer individual, characterful faces and naturally drawn
hair to polished modern fantasy glamour. Avoid anime or manga facial proportions,
sleek anime-prince styling and glossy digital rendering.

Characters may be young, beautiful or smooth-skinned: the antique quality comes
from the drawing and paint, not automatically from wrinkles or a weathered face.
The eagle-mounted Air Consort is a useful reference for this stronger old-tarot
direction. Preserve simple colour masses rather than adding pervasive distressed
texture or uniform sepia.

Weapons must be physically coherent and clearly held: one continuous shaft or
blade axis, a connected grip and guard where applicable, and a closed hand visibly
wrapping the handle. Simplify or change the gesture if a weapon cannot be drawn
clearly. Riding poses must show credible contact between rider, saddle and mount.

## Characters and emotion

Every character needs a particular personality and an unmistakable emotional
state: tortured, victorious, mischievous, anguished, ecstatic, defiant, troubled,
or another strongly felt condition. Convey it through face, posture, hands and
the surrounding scene together.

Prefer interesting, quirky people to generic fantasy heroes. Asymmetry, awkward
grace, weathered faces, distinctive clothing and unexpected gestures can make a
character memorable. Beauty, age, gender and body type may vary across the deck.
Hierarchy titles are gender neutral.

### Wartime standing

This is a world at war. Rank should be legible through bearing, equipment and
readiness as well as fine clothing. The Consort is rank 12, second-highest in
each elemental suit, and must carry credible martial or strategic authority.
Do not reduce high-ranking characters to passive decorative companions.
For the Air Consort, include a visible weapon and a commanding, alert presence.
Across the deck, use weapons, armour or other concrete signs of wartime duty
where appropriate to the character; keep them integrated and readable without
overloading the silhouette.

Choose one dominant emotion and, where useful, one conflicting undertone:
triumph with exhaustion, mischief with desperation, authority with doubt.
Quirkiness should reveal character rather than accumulate unrelated props.

Match costume condition to the character's story and standing. Eccentricity does
not automatically mean rags: fine tailoring, unusual cuts and restrained ornament
can convey personality. Use tears, patches and distressed edges only deliberately.

## Composition and storytelling

- Portrait artwork, approximately 5:7; the current reference size is 1060 × 1484 px.
- One dominant character, a clear silhouette, and a readable face and gesture.
- Full-length figures where the scene benefits; closer framing is also valid.
- Give the character one clear action and a small number of meaningful objects.
- Make the landscape participate in the mood and elemental identity.
- Reserve quiet space in the upper-right corner for card labels added later.
  Currently only this corner needs clearance; the upper-left may carry artwork.
  As a working starting point, keep the rightmost 22% of the width and upper
  25% of the height free of faces, props, strong contours and busy texture.
  Continue a simple sky or other calm background through this area; do not
  draw boxes or numerals into the illustration.
- Check the composition at thumbnail size, especially face, hands and suit motif.
- Generate full-bleed artwork without baked-in text, rank numbers or frames.
  Card labels and interface marks are applied separately according to the hierarchy.

## Elemental motifs

Keep each card's palette simple and strongly tied to its element. Carry that
colour family into the sky and surroundings. Limit competing accent colours,
group background detail into quieter masses, and reserve the clearest contrasts
for the face, gesture, key prop and suit motif. The image must read at small size.

Use [the symbol sheet](symbols-air-fire-water-earth.png) as the authority.
Preserve each symbol's four-part structure and orientation.

| Suit | Motif | Colour family | Possible surroundings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air | Four straight horizontal bars | White, ivory, silver, slate | Wind, high ground, cloud, open sky |
| Fire | Four vertical wavy strokes | Red, ember, orange, gold | Heat, volcanic rock, flame, fierce light |
| Water | Four horizontal waves | Blue, cyan, deep sea tones | Shore, river, rain, sea |
| Earth | Four stacked angular zigzag bands | Green, moss, ochre, soil | Forest, stone, roots, cultivated land |

Earth's main greens should be rich forest and emerald hues, with clear deep
green colour masses. Avoid letting the character and costume drift entirely
into yellow-olive, muddy brown or pale desaturated green.

Integrate the motif into something physical: embroidery, collar, tattoo, shield,
carved stone, jewellery or a tool. It should belong to the character's world and
remain clearly legible. Do not substitute a generic elemental icon.
The motif may be rendered in a material such as carved stone while the broader
palette establishes the suit.

## Hierarchy and identity

[card-hierarchy.md](card-hierarchy.md) defines the ranks and game behaviour.
Each element has ranks 1–13: Peasant, Farmer, Citizen, Soldier, Merchant, Priest,
Trickster, Seer, Noble, General, Heir, Consort, Monarch.
Jester and Wizard belong to no element.

Trickster is rank 7 and Seer rank 8. Develop a shared archetype for each, then
express it differently through the four suits. A general concept study may leave
the suit unresolved; a final ranked card must establish its suit.

### Different characters across suits

The same rank in all four suits must depict substantially DIFFERENT characters,
not one design recoloured four times. Share the rank's idea and the deck's drawing
style, not its face, species, costume, silhouette, pose, prop or setting.
Vary age, build, species, expression and visual storytelling deliberately.
Give each its own outfit and action in a location strongly shaped by its element.
Do not repeat a signature prop such as the Water Trickster's tragedy mask.

Before generating a set, compare the four briefs: each must be recognisable in
silhouette and through its action even with the suit colours removed. References
from another suit are for paint, ink and simplicity only; explicitly prohibit
copying their character, pose, costume, props and location.

For the Tricksters, retain the selected Water confidence artist with the mask.
Fire should be somewhat demonic, related in species to the demon player mesh;
Earth should be a green-skinned elf related to the forest-elf player species.
Air needs an independently conceived character and visual trick. These are
different people expressing cunning through their own elemental world.

Keep Trickster distinct from the existing carefree Fool/Jester reference:
the Trickster acts knowingly, manipulating expectations or appearances.
Existing filenames such as knight, queen and prophet are development references,
not replacements for the current hierarchy titles.

## Working process

1. Read the hierarchy and choose the rank, concept and suit (or explicitly mark
   a study as suit undecided).
2. Write a short proposal: dominant emotion, action, silhouette, meaningful
   object, elemental integration and background.
3. Select a few references with explicit roles: style, motif, composition.
4. Generate an illustration using the style direction above. Keep labels out
   of the painted image.
5. Review emotion, silhouette, anatomy, motif fidelity and consistency with
   the deck. Correct the most important mismatch first.
6. Save new versions without replacing earlier artwork. Keep the generation
   prompt beside the image.
7. Use photocraft-cli for deterministic crops, transparency, resizing, simple
   shapes and export. Use image generation for illustrated scene/style changes.

## Review checklist

- Does the character have a memorable emotional life?
- Do pose, expression and setting tell the same story?
- Are painterly colour and dark ink drawing both visible?
- Does it avoid photographic or polished 3D realism?
- Is the suit motif accurate, integrated and readable?
- Is the hierarchy role clear and distinct from neighbouring roles?
- Does the image still work at playing-card size?
- Are colour masses coherent and the upper-right label area visually quiet?
- Is this character clearly different from the other three suits of the same
  rank in species/identity, outfit, pose, prop and elemental location?
