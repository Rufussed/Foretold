# Trickster — first concept proposal

Status: proposal, not yet generated.
Rank: 7. Initial archetype study; suit undecided.

## Recommended concept: The False Lament

A travelling confidence artist is caught in the instant their sorrowful
performance slips. They lift a small carved tragedy mask from their face.
Above the mask, a raised eyebrow and crooked, almost victorious smile reveal
that the apparent grief was calculated. A trace of fatigue in the eyes keeps
the character human and slightly unsettling.

Their body remains bent in theatrical lament while their face betrays delight:
one hand raises the mask, the other opens in an exaggerated plea.
A narrow, angular silhouette, expressive long fingers, a patched but once-fine
coat, and one extravagantly turned collar give them an eccentric presence.
No pointed fool's hat, bells, cliff-edge stroll or accompanying dog.

Set them in a windswept, nearly deserted market square at dusk, with a leaning
curtain and a few distant architectural shapes suggesting an improvised stage.
Keep the background subordinate to the face, mask and gesture.

The deception of appearances connects to the Trickster's influence over
predictions. It need not literally illustrate the rules with numerals or game UI.

## Elemental development

For the study, explore costume and expression without adding all four symbols.
Before making a final card, choose one suit:

- **Air:** ivory and slate coat, four white bars embroidered on the turned collar;
  wind lifts the curtain and coat tails.
- **Fire:** vermilion lining, four flame strokes stitched into a lapel;
  dusk becomes ember-lit and the smile more brazen.
- **Water:** deep blue costume, four waves on the mask's handle or cuff;
  wet paving and reflected light echo the false tears.
- **Earth:** moss-green coat, four angular bands carved into a wooden mask;
  a root-buckled square and hanging ivy suggest patient, practiced deception.

Water is a promising first suit: false tears, reflections and a changeable face
give it a clear visual story. Keep the motif on a cuff if carving it into the
mask would compete with the mask's expression.

## Other directions worth considering

- **The Borrowed Crown:** a ragged impostor adjusts an absurdly grand crown,
  visibly delighted and terrified by their own success. Strong silhouette;
  distinguish carefully from Monarch and Jester.
- **The Shadow Cheat:** a charming figure makes an innocent gesture while
  their painted shadow performs a different one. More uncanny and symbolic;
  the contradictory shadow must remain readable at small size.

Tarot-inspired painterly illustration with confident irregular dark ink
outlines, tactile opaque brushwork, slightly exaggerated human features,
expressive posture, simplified folds and atmospheric painted surroundings.
